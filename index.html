      Method

      How the numbers work

      League scoring
      1. Per-game valueESPN projects each player's fantasy points per game under this league's exact scoring. After a player has 5 games this season, his real average starts to count too, up to 60% of the number by game 25.
      2. AvailabilityOut or suspended counts as 0. Day-to-day and questionable count at 85%. Doubtful counts at 30%.
      3. Daily best lineupFor every remaining day of the week, each team's players with a game are fitted into the 11 starting slots (PG, SG, SF, PF, C, G, F, 4 UTIL) to get the most points. Leftovers sit.
      4. Week projectionPoints already scored this week plus the sum of those daily best lineups. More games and fewer empty slots means more points.
      5. Win chanceEach start gets an uncertainty of about 35% of the player's value, and Each remaining start can swing about 45% around the player's projection. Each team also carries 12% of its remaining projection for things that don't average out (minutes changes, rest days, injuries), day-to-day players count as a real chance of a zero, and open lineup spots count as room for the other side to stream. Those numbers come from how far real weeks have landed from projection. The margin is compared on a fat-tailed curve because bad nights come in bunches. It only shows 100% or 0% once there's nothing left to play.
      6. Power scorePer-game value of a team's top 11, plus a quarter of the bench, minus 2 per injury tag. It measures roster strength, not this week's schedule.
      7. MovesThe top 40 free agents are swapped in for each of your low-value players, and the week is replayed. Swaps that add the most points are listed. Technical-foul magnets are skipped.
